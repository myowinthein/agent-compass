## Eligibility before grouping

Every included portal must have current IT/tech listings relevant to the searched country, regardless of its global, regional or specialist reach. Verify actual country/category/search/job pages and retain a useful official deep link and evidence date. A global tech board with fresh jobs only elsewhere does not qualify. Remote listings count only when the target country is explicitly an eligible work location; a remote label alone is insufficient.

Discover broadly across software, DevOps/SRE, infrastructure, networking, database administration, QA/testing, security, data/AI, IT support, product and UX/UI/design where relevant portals exist. Do not narrow discovery to development because the user is a developer. An individual specialist board can serve one discipline; it need not cover every IT role.

Exclude aggregation-focused job-search engines that primarily collect or republish external vacancies. Explicitly exclude Indeed and its country editions, even though it also accepts direct employer posts. Prefer a portal's own employer, recruiter or community posting ecosystem. When its model is unclear, inspect its own posting/about pages and observed jobs; an external employer application link, ATS feed or cross-posted vacancy alone does not prove aggregation. Do not attempt a vacancy-by-vacancy overlap audit or promise zero duplication across independent boards. Merge redirects and rebrands of the same portal into one entry; shared ownership alone does not make distinct boards the same portal.

Omit unqualified candidates quietly: aggregators, stale or expired-only results, broken/unusable portals, unverifiable country coverage and services without actual job listings. Ordinary account creation to apply/save does not itself disqualify a portal when current country-relevant listings and a usable application route can be established. Account access and a particular job's legal eligibility are separate facts; never generalize one vacancy's sponsorship or nationality condition to the whole portal.

If a relevant official page fails, try another relevant public page on the same domain. If coverage still cannot be verified, omit the portal from the live list without calling it dead or inactive. Preserve other verified results. Do not print rejected-site lists, failed-check details or per-portal warning sections.

## Exactly three groups

Use these labels, in this order, with each portal appearing once:

1. Global job boards
2. Local & regional job boards
3. IT & tech niche job boards

Classify IT/tech-specialist boards or specialist job communities as Niche first, whether global or local. Split remaining multi-career boards by their actual market reach: Global serves many countries; Local & regional focuses on one country or a small set of markets. A global board's country edition stays Global. A country's domain suffix or the company's headquarters alone does not determine reach.

Remote-focused boards follow the same rule: IT-only goes in Niche; multi-career goes in Global or Local & regional according to reach. A professional network or community qualifies through its usable jobs section, not community activity alone. Recruitment agencies with actual searchable jobs and public employment-service boards may fit these same groups when they meet the coverage/access rules; ownership alone neither qualifies nor excludes them. Add no recruitment-agency, community, government, remote or other fourth group. Omit empty groups rather than fill them with irrelevant sites.

## Useful entries and stars

For each portal give its name with the official URL immediately after, its global/regional/country scope, and one compact sentence explaining its practical strength. Prefer the useful country/jobs page. Avoid company/vacancy anecdotes, marketing copy, generic feature lists and job-level caveats that do not explain portal usefulness.

Within each group, ⭐ identifies the strongest supported starting point. Start with a clear standout; star additional portals only when observed strengths are genuinely comparable. There is no fixed star count or 2–3-per-group quota. A sole verified entry is not automatically a recommended pick; leave entries unstarred when the evidence does not justify that judgment.

Compare observed current country-relevant coverage, depth/breadth within the portal's stated focus, freshness, useful search/location filters and clear working job-detail/application routes. Evaluate niche boards within their specialties rather than favoring development or demanding coverage of every IT career. One isolated vacancy establishes limited coverage, not automatically a top recommendation. Base stars on opened evidence rather than reputation, raw incomparable job counts or invented scores. The short description should convey the practical strength supporting a star without a separate scoring report.

## Verification limits and output

For live results, include one concise "Verified as of [date]" line naming the country. If access genuinely limited research, mark partial coverage briefly in that line. Intentional exclusions are filtering, not proof of incomplete coverage. Keep the main list to usable qualifying portals, without ⚠️ entries or an appendix of failed sites. Do not conceal a material usability problem just to keep a portal; omit it. Ordinary account requirements need no warning catalogue.

If nothing qualifies, give a brief no-verified-results statement with the overall limitation instead of a misleading empty success. When browsing is unavailable, follow the shared access/supplied-evidence fallback; do not invent a live result. An explicitly requested non-live assessment of supplied material retains its evidence date and "user-supplied; not live verified" label, without stars implying live verification. Supplied claims are not permission to present an unestablished portal as a live recommendation.
