# Country assessment rules

Apply in Country Finder discovery, research, scoring and final audit. The goal is to decide where this person should spend job-search effort. Country attractiveness, a legal work route and a suitable current vacancy are different findings.

## Compare the same factors

Research every selected country on both tracks unless an explicit, supported exclusion applies to that track. For each non-excluded track retain these findings in its research, with sources, reference periods, geographical scope and material unknowns:

- **Market demand:** relevant occupation and job hubs; recurring opportunities across employers; hiring trends, recruitment difficulty, shortages or surpluses and available competition evidence. Map official occupations by duties and specialization, not title alone. A broad technology shortage does not establish demand for every engineering stack or design role.
- **Role match:** the person's skills, seniority, domain experience, qualifications and required working language against the opportunity set. Distinguish close matches from reasonable transitions and materially different roles. Existing relevant overseas employment or professional connections can identify useful channels, but historical access does not prove present recruitment.
- **International access:** applicable employer policies and relevant hiring/sponsoring examples, separately from the legal route. Remote requires evidence applicable to the actual working location; global or regional eligibility can qualify without naming that country literally when exclusions and role restrictions are checked. Relocation needs the candidate-specific route and employer willingness. Sponsor registration proves capability only; silence in one advert proves neither refusal nor support.
- **Pay feasibility:** comparable role/level/location pay and the confirmed minimum, with employment versus contractor basis kept explicit. Apply the salary-comparison rules; do not infer an employee-only preference or accept contractor revenue as gross base. Missing published pay in one vacancy leaves that amount unknown, not the whole market. Local employee benchmarks can inform relocation feasibility, never substitute for cross-border Remote pay or certify an offer.
- **Practical constraints:** working language, qualification recognition where relevant, timezone, relocation timing and substantiated route/citizenship restrictions. Consider cost of living, family, career prospects or quality of life only where relevant to confirmed preferences; do not start an extra lifestyle intake or override a hard salary floor with lower living costs.

For a firm relocation deadline, compare researched route processing and known prerequisites with that date/timeframe. Retain supported market/role fit, but carry a supported timing mismatch into Relocation priority/order, its limitation and next action; that route cannot receive Focus for the stated deadline. An unresolved material timing condition needs verification. Processing estimates alone do not guarantee an offer or start date. "No rush", no fixed deadline and unspecified timing add no timing penalty. Timing never excludes Remote. Distinguish user-confirmed personal immigration facts from general citizenship/employer-risk claims; the latter need applicable research before affecting fit, access or confidence.

Use independent market and employer evidence together. Prefer official occupation outlooks, shortage analysis and employment/wage trends for market claims; verify employer policies and representative current vacancies for accessibility and fit. Credible specialist surveys/recruiters can fill narrower gaps with methodology and dates disclosed. Search snippets, duplicated adverts and generic best-country rankings do not establish market depth. Raw vacancy counts across different boards/countries are not comparable market rankings. One opening is a lead, not a deep market; a closed role or one employer's refusal does not establish a country-wide lack of opportunity.

A shortage is a positive demand signal, not proof of sponsorship, good pay or a personal hiring probability. Distinguish an immigration occupation list from a statistical shortage assessment and state the date/category. An unavailable or absent list is unknown, not evidence of surplus.

## Research gaps

Discovery is a first pass. Its `yes` means sufficient grounds for investigation, not confirmed eligibility or a complete offer. `no evidence found` also proceeds to detailed research; it is not a filter. Reserve `no` for an explicit supported track exclusion, retaining its exact reason. Missing discovery results require recovery before proceeding.

Detailed research must report which factors were checked, sources opened, useful leads and unresolved questions in each track's **Research coverage** field. Distinguish no relevant findings after substantive searching, inaccessible sources and work not performed. An honestly reported unknown is a field value; an omitted field or failed task is incomplete.

Before completing Step 3, give promising tracks with decision-changing gaps a targeted follow-up within the confirmed country/person/track scope. Use a fresh brief that names the gap and already checked leads, seeks a different relevant source class or concrete employer policy, and preserves the same candidate facts. The researcher's literal brief may carry these prior findings for verification; it still cannot browse workspace files or other tasks. Append the returned follow-up to that track's original aggregate block before ingestion, clearly separating original findings from newly dated evidence and retaining contradictions. Do not silently replace original claims, loop the same query until a positive answer appears or expand the country list. No automatic contact with employers or recruiters.

A credible demand/role signal with unclear pay, sponsorship or location terms warrants this follow-up. Where the first pass found no such signal, still complete the consistent detailed checks before reporting an unresolved market. Further recovery is needed when the research was not actually performed or access failed; use the runtime's existing retry/manual remedy. After substantive first and applicable targeted follow-up searches, unavailable or unpublished facts can remain unresolved with their next verification action. Later scoring/audit does not conduct new research or rewrite stored evidence; material new information returns to the parent for the existing repair choice.

## Assess fit separately from verification

In Step 5 retain market demand, role match, international access, pay feasibility, practical constraints and research coverage as concise evidence-linked prose per track. Assign Strong / Moderate / Weak fit only from stored applicable research. Strong needs converging role-relevant market and employer evidence; a single lead supports at most a conditional Moderate fit with appropriate confidence. Weak needs supported poor match or limited opportunity, not missing facts. No usable demand/role evidence remains `not scored (no data)`.

Unknown access or pay does not erase supported demand and role fit. Record the condition explicitly and use Investigate next rather than claiming an application-ready recommendation. Preserve actual exclusions, independent tracks and exact salary comparison rules. Confidence measures the support for the assessment, not offer probability; use High / Medium / Low only for scored fits, and Not assessed for missing/excluded fits.

For Step 6's typed presentation decisions use:

- **Access:** Supported when applicable policy or relevant hiring evidence supports the path; Unverified for a material unresolved condition; Blocked only for a concrete exclusion. Neither a general visa route nor an EOR provider's country coverage alone establishes employer willingness.
- **Pay:** Supported when comparable evidence supports feasible pay at the confirmed floor; Not specified when no hard floor applies; Unverified for missing/incompatible evidence or a straddling range; Below floor only when a valid comparison puts the entire relevant pay range below the personal hard floor. Apply Step 5's track-specific consequence: Remote exclusion or evidence-supported Weak relocation feasibility, not an invented legal block. An informational visa-pay comparison alone cannot establish personal pay failure. Supported is market feasibility, not a guaranteed offer.

Priority is independent for each track: Focus / Selective / Monitor require Strong or Moderate fit, supported access and supported pay feasibility (or no hard floor). Investigate next requires a supported Strong/Moderate fit with material access or pay still unverified. Deprioritize applies to that track's Weak or Excluded fit even if the other track is unknown. Pending evidence applies to an unscored track after research/recovery accounting; never rank those countries as if their opportunity were established. Do not mechanically score or weight the factors, invent probabilities, or fill a quota of recommended countries.

## Basis for the framework

These sources inform the factors, not live country findings or a universal ranking formula. Open current applicable sources during each search:

- [ILO occupational classification](https://ilostat.ilo.org/methods/concepts-and-definitions/classification-occupation/): duties, skill level and specialization.
- [OECD shortage methodology](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/mapping-skill-shortages-in-oecd-countries_735f9485-en/full-report/component-5.html): multiple demand signals and vacancy-data limitations.
- [OECD talent-attractiveness methodology](https://www.oecd.org/en/data/tools/talent-attractiveness-research--methodology.html): opportunities, personal circumstances and accessibility; its skilled-worker access comparison assumes an existing suitable offer.
- [Oxford international job-search guidance](https://www.ox.ac.uk/careers/careers-guidance/job-search-and-applications/job-search-strategies/finding-jobs-outside-the-uk): sector, language, qualifications and networks.
- [Job Bank labour-market tools](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/trend-analysis?wbdisable=true): occupation- and location-specific wages and outlooks.
